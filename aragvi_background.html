Crush margin is the spread between raw sunflower seed cost and the combined revenue from refined oil and meal. Historically ranges from $60/MT (stress) to $140/MT (peak), averaging $90/MT across Aragvi's 1.2M MT crushing fleet.

⚔️ 7. Deep Dive: Creditor-on-Creditor Violence vs Bank Syndicate Cooperation Game Theory HoldCo Eurobonds ($397.5M Senior Notes & Lines) vs OpCo Trade Finance & DFI Banks ($267.6M PXF & Term Mortgages)
In an emerging market agribusiness distress situation, the primary threat to offshore bondholders is rarely raw asset inadequacy, but rather creditor-on-creditor violence: international commodity trade finance banks (ING, Rabobank, Société Générale, UniCredit) holding senior pledges over grain inventories, warehouse receipts, and export receivables seizing physical collateral, shutting off working capital, and starving HoldCo bondholders into accepting coercive haircuts. For Aragvi Holding, analyzing whether trade finance banks and multilateral DFIs (EBRD, BSTDB, FMO, IFC) will initiate warfare or cooperate is the single most critical determinant of secondary bond pricing and restructuring recovery.
1. Capital Stack Hierarchy STRUCTURAL PRIORITY

HoldCo Eurobond investors are structurally subordinated to $267.6M of operating priority debt:

  • Pre-Export Finance (PXF) Revolvers ($186.5M drawn): First-lien security over grain inventories in 16 silos, export contracts, and Swiss collection accounts. Lenders: ING, Rabobank, UniCredit, SocGen.
  • Multilateral DFI Term Loans ($81.1M): Secured mortgages over Giurgiulești port docks and Bălți extraction machinery. Lenders: EBRD, BSTDB, FMO, IFC.
  • HoldCo Senior Eurobonds ($243.4M 12.15% 2026 + $100M 12.75% 2031): Unsecured direct obligations of Aragvi Holding Ltd. (Cyprus/Switzerland), pari passu with $54.1M drawn bank credit lines.
  • Unencumbered Assets: Țăndărei crushing plant in Romania (EU asset) and Danube barge fleet (under-pledged).
2. Potential Creditor Violence DOWNGRADE THREATS

How secured commodity lenders could aggressively pressure HoldCo Eurobond holders:

  • PXF Revolver Freeze & Inventory Seizure (-4.5c drag): Trade finance banks could refuse to roll seasonal procurement facilities upon bond maturity default, enforcing warehouse liens and liquidating grain stocks at distressed auction prices.
  • Structural Priming on Swiss Trading Arm (-3.5c drag): Banks could demand exclusive first-priority share pledges over Aragvi Trading SA (Switzerland), cutting off upstream cash flows to the Cyprus holding entity.
  • Coercive Debt-for-Equity Restructuring: Senior secured lenders could team up with sponsor Vaja Jhashi to implement an English Scheme of Arrangement or restructuring plan that crams down bondholders with steep 30%+ haircuts.
3. Why Banks Choose Cooperation DOMINANT STRATEGY

Four systemic forces compel commercial banks and DFIs to support a consensual maturity extension:

  • The EBRD / BSTDB DFI Governance Anchor: EBRD and BSTDB are core lenders committed to Moldovan economic survival. They strictly oppose predatory liquidation and mediate consensual standstill agreements.
  • Perishable Agricultural Value Destruction: Seizing raw sunflower seeds and uncrushed grain halts processing, destroys seasonal trading relationships, and triggers immediate commercial value destruction for all claimants.
  • The EU "Solidarity Lanes" Imperative: Giurgiulești port and Aragvi's barge fleet are designated vital transit corridors by the European Commission. Western governments will not permit bank foreclosure that closes the port.
  • Self-Liquidating Nature of PXF Lines: PXF debt naturally self-liquidates as harvests are sold into hard-currency export contracts; commercial banks have no economic reason to force a default.
