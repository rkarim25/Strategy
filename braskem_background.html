The 4 Primary Drivers of Braskem's EV Volatility

1. The Global Petrochemical Spread Super-Cycle: Polyethylene (PE) cash spreads over naphtha fluctuate between historical troughs of $150–$250/t (seen in 2023–2024 following massive Chinese capacity additions) and peak levels of $700–$850/t (2021 post-pandemic restocking). Because Braskem operates with huge physical volume (~8.5Mt polymers), a mere $50/ton spread variance translates to a $380M swing in EBITDA.

2. High Operating Leverage: Petrochemical crackers are high fixed-cost, continuous 24/7 industrial infrastructure. Once volume covers fixed cash plant overhead, additional margin drops straight to EBITDA, magnifying EV volatility by 3.5x–4.0x relative to underlying chemical price moves.

3. Petrobras Feedstock Formula Risk: ~70% of Braskem's domestic naphtha is purchased from Petrobras under contracts indexed to international ARA (Amsterdam-Rotterdam-Antwerp) benchmarks. Political shifts or negotiations regarding the domestic discount/parity can swing Braskem's annual feedstock bill by $150M–$250M overnight.
